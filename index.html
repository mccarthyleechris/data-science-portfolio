About

Electric vehicles · Regression

Predicting Electric Vehicle Driving Range

Question
Which vehicle specs best predict how far an EV can drive on one charge?
Approach
Used 2025 specifications for 478 EV models. Compared a simple regression on battery capacity alone with a multiple regression that adds efficiency, torque and vehicle segment, then checked the residuals.
Result
Battery capacity alone explains 77% of the variation in range. The full model explains 93% (R² 0.93) and nearly halves the average error, from 49 km to 26 km.

Electric vehicles · Classification

Washington EV Adoption Trends and Manufacturer Classification

Question
How has EV adoption grown in Washington, and can a vehicle's traits predict whether it comes from a high-adoption manufacturer?
Approach
Explored about 270,000 registered EVs: growth by model year, the battery-electric versus plug-in hybrid split, and manufacturer share. Engineered features and trained decision tree and random forest classifiers.
Result
The random forest reached 81% accuracy and a single decision tree reached 77%, against a 64% baseline from always guessing the majority class. In the tree, whether a car is fully battery-electric was the most important feature.

Data engineering · Wrangling

EV and Charging Station Data Pipeline

Question
How do EV counts compare with charging infrastructure across Washington cities?
Approach
Combined three sources into one SQLite database: the state's EV registration file, a federal table of EV laws and incentives scraped from the Alternative Fuels Data Center, and charging station data from the OpenChargeMap API. Each source was cleaned in five documented steps before merging.
Result
A single queryable database and charts comparing EV counts, charging stations and charge points by city.
